Development process

A human–AI development timeline. This page is a summary of agent-development-log.md and does not add anything that is not in it. Failures and unfinished work are included on purpose.

Josie (design decisions) · Claude Opus 5 via Claude Code (implementation) · 5–6 September 2026

Timeline

  1. 5 September 2026 · Interaction 01

    Indexing the workspace, before any design

    The AI was told to read everything first and write no code. It listed the three files in the folder, read the brief and the log protocol, and read the hand-drawn system graph.

    It reported ten contradictions and gaps rather than smoothing over them. The three that mattered:

    • The brief file is named domain-learning-brief.md, but section 11 of the brief expects brief.md.
    • The power of the pot is listed as both environment data and player-controlled data. It cannot be both.
    • The brief says there are "about 6 doneness levels" but only ever names about four of them.

    No design decisions were made at this step. Gaps were reported, not filled.

  2. 6 September 2026 · Interaction 02

    The student closes the design gaps

    The AI separated seven blocking questions from four that could wait, and Josie answered all seven: the six English doneness names, hold-to-cook and release-to-take-out, the player controls the heat, an endless run scored in coins, 8–12 second patience on Easy and 4–8 on Hard, two presets only, and no timer.

    In a second pass she removed the coin penalty completely — a customer either pays or does not — and added the constraint that ended up shaping the whole build:

    Difficulty must never change how fast the egg cooks. Cooking speed depends only on heat and egg size. This is what stops Easy and Hard from being a difficulty slider: they change the constraints you work under, not the physics.

    Where AI pushed back

    Josie asked for a patience countdown. The AI flagged that a numeric countdown would hand the player a clock, and since cooking speed is fixed, players would learn "deliver at 5 seconds left" and stop reading the water entirely — defeating the learning shift the whole project is built on. Josie chose a draining bar with no digits.

    Where AI added things that were not in the brief

    Recorded honestly, because they are the AI's ideas and not Josie's:

    • Splitting the two visual cues. The brief lists "how the water boils" and "how the egg moves" as feedback but never says they are separate channels. The AI proposed that the water tells you about the pot while the egg's movement tells you about the egg — so a violent boil shakes even a raw egg and you have to see past it. Presented as an interpretation; not objected to.
    • Orders never ask for Raw or Overcooked, since those read as failure states rather than things a customer orders. The brief does not say this.
  3. 6 September 2026 · Interaction 03

    First playable milestone — game and website together

    Built in one pass: the complete loop, the six-level ladder, three heat levels, both presets, the coin rules, game over, a per-difficulty best score, and this website. Dependency track A — plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Canvas 2D. The boiling sound is generated live by the Web Audio API, so nothing is loaded from a CDN and the game works offline.

    Other decisions the AI made on its own

    • Three discrete heat levels instead of a continuous dial — the brief only says "high / low heat".
    • Heat carries over between customers, so you can preheat for the next order.
    • A fairness guard: an order is re-rolled if it could not be reached at full heat with 1.2 seconds to spare. Without it, a large egg with a hard-boiled order and 4 seconds of patience would be impossible. It re-rolls the order, never the cooking speed.
    • Releasing Space serves the egg immediately, making lifting and handing over one gesture. This means you cannot lift the egg out to inspect it — a real consequence Josie has not signed off on yet.
    • The water visibly boils away over time, from the brief's line about long evaporation meaning overcooked.

    What went wrong

    The first attempt to write assets/js/game.js was corrupted mid-write: a stray fragment of text landed in the middle of the file and about a third of the functions never got written. The AI caught it, said so, and rewrote the file with a different tool instead of patching around the damage.

    What is verified, and what is not

    Verified: all three scripts parse, the pages load, the loop runs end to end, and every path in the project is relative.

    Not verified: nobody has actually played it yet. Two things are genuinely uncertain — whether the egg's spin stays readable against a hard boil on Hard difficulty, and whether 4-second patience feels fair or just frantic.

  4. 6 September 2026 · Interaction 04

    Playtest note: the pot was not actually boiling

    Josie played the first build and said it was wrong: the bubbles and the sound should climb from small to loud over time, and the egg's turning should get more and more obvious.

    She was right, and for two reasons the AI had missed:

    • The water snapped straight to whatever the heat dial said and then sat flat. There was no build-up at all, so the sound had nothing to climb.
    • The egg only rotated on its own axis — and a near-symmetrical oval rotating in place looks almost identical to one standing still.

    What changed: the pot now has to work up to a rolling boil, faster on high heat, and a cold egg knocks it back down when it goes in — a large egg further than a small one. The egg now circles around the pot instead of spinning in place, with a wake behind it, and it has speckles on the shell so the turning is actually visible. The sound climbs with the boil and keeps opening the longer the egg is in.

    This made the feedback closer to the brief, not further from it: section 4 says the sound goes "from low to loud" to show how long it has boiled, and that the egg "starts to turn around in the pot". Neither was really true of the first build.

    Verified: a headless test harness now drives the real game files and checks the boil actually dips when the egg goes in and then climbs, and that the egg's turning accelerates as it cooks. 16 checks passing.

  5. 6 September 2026 · Interaction 05

    Second playtest note: the ramp still didn't start from calm

    Josie played the fixed build and said the ripples should also go from small to large, and the sound wasn't quiet enough at the start.

    She was right again. The previous fix had built the ramp mechanic, but left three separate hard floors in three different files, each quietly keeping the pot at a medium starting point instead of near-zero: the agitation target had a 40% floor even at a cold pot, the ripple drawing had a fixed baseline wobble regardless of agitation, and the sound's noise bed had a fixed minimum volume. All three were removed, and the build-up now eases in on a curve rather than a straight line, so the first second reads as noticeably calmer than the last.

    Verified: extended the same test harness — agitation now measures under 0.05 the instant a fresh pot appears, and ramps smoothly through 0.042 → 0.069 → 0.267 → 0.550 over the next few seconds. 18 of 18 checks passing.

Still open

  • Playtesting. Nobody outside the project has played it.
  • Is the egg's turning readable at high heat, or does the shaking water still hide it? The circling motion and the shell speckles were added to fix this, but nobody has played the new build yet.
  • Should serving stay tied to releasing Space, or should lifting the egg out and handing it over be two separate actions?
  • "Easy" and "Hard" are generic names for two presets that differ in a specific way. They probably deserve better names.
  • No background music yet — only the boiling sound. The brief asks for "cute music".
  • No stage reflection has been written yet. Josie asked to be the one to trigger it.
  • Whether this folder becomes its own repository for GitHub Pages, and whether the brief and the development log may be published publicly.
