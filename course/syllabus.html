AI-Assisted Game Design Engine

A fourteen-week research studio for turning guided design thinking into a structured blueprint, a minimum playable demo, and an evidence-driven design loop—without handing authorship to the agent.

Monday · 8:30–11:00 14 weeks 10–12 hours / week 3 testable sprints Browser-based engine
01 · Starting point

From teaching pipelines to a design engine

The study grows from two summer teaching designs in Game Design from Life. Both use guiding questions to produce an AI-agent-ready development outline and then a minimum playable demo. This semester asks what must become explicit in the tool itself.

When an AI agent joins game creation, what interaction structure and design language let a novice complete guide → structure → playable verification while retaining design authorship?
Summer path A

Emotion Experience

Lived event → emotional structure → variables and challenge space → playable mechanics.

Summer path B

Domain Learning

Novice/expert gap → skill loop and feedback → challenge space → playable system.

02 · Theory

Four lenses, one negotiable scaffold

The layers are critique lenses, not four sequential products. L1–L3 become progressively visible across the sprints; L4 and P-Scaffold are observed and protected throughout.

L1

Game Design Structure

MDA and design patterns provide vocabulary without becoming a forced linear method.

Structure is the basic language.

Mixed initiative, agency, authorship, and boundary objects define who proposes and who decides.

03 · Development

A 4–3–2 sprint architecture

The nine build weeks are intentionally unequal. The structural substrate receives four weeks; the working contract receives three; closure and evidence use a focused two-week sprint. Every sprint ends with an external designer-user test.

Sprint 01
4 weeks

Structure as language

A designer-user can enter anywhere, complete a shared blueprint, see unresolved structure, and compile a minimum demo. AI may compile; it may not silently redesign.

Blueprint SSOTAny entry Visible open slotsMinimum demo
Test 1 · Week 7 · n=1–2

Can a stranger read and fill the blueprint? Does the resulting demo still express their structure?

Sprint 02
3 weeks

The human–AI working contract

The blueprint remains authoritative while the agent becomes an active proposer. Suggestions, committed decisions, provenance, and undo must be legible in the interface.

Suggestion railConfirm to write ProvenanceUndo
Test 2 · Week 10 · n=2–3

Can users tell suggestion from decision? Can they point to what they—not the model—committed?

Sprint 03
2 weeks

Verification closes the loop

Evaluation returns to the blueprint as design feedback. This sprint is closure and evidence, not a new platform: reuse the summer questions for gaps, run health, and intent alignment.

Structure checkRun health Intent probeEvidence-led revision
Test 3 · Weeks 11–12 · n=5–8

Does evaluation reveal what is missing or off? At least one engine change must trace to evidence.

05 · Evidence

What the study leaves behind

The engine is the central artifact. Documentation and testing make its decisions inspectable rather than ornamental.

01

Deployed engine

Browser-accessible tool, repository, and a short rationale explaining blueprint, working contract, and verification loop.

02

Research trail

Living design document, weekly iteration logs, three protocols, and anonymized observations showing accept / modify / reject decisions.

03

Testing report

A 2,500–4,000 word comparison: question, method, findings across three increments, design implications, and limitations.

Week 14 · NYU Shanghai

A public research release, not just a final presentation

A concise research talk, a stable live demo, an optional hands-on design task, and an open discussion. The goal is not to claim completion; it is to expose the engine’s assumptions to people who see systems, interaction, and lived experience differently.
